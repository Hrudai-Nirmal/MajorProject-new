--- RISKS (flagged by Motley Fool editorial) ---
A January network outage cut wireless service revenue by approximately 80 basis points in the quarter via customer credits; CFO Anthony Skiadas described this as "a onetime pressure... related to our network outage" and confirmed no lingering financial impact was expected. Wireless service revenue itself declined 1% year over year to $20.6 billion, pressured by customer credits and "elevated promotional amortization" the company is still lapping from prior-year pricing actions (about 180 basis points). Net unsecured debt to adjusted EBITDA rose to approximately 2.6x due to the Frontier acquisition, with the company targeting 2.0-2.25x only by 2027. The CFO also disclosed $1.1 billion in severance payments absorbed during the quarter as part of restructuring.

--- TAKEAWAYS ---
Total revenue was $34.4 billion, up 2.9% year over year. Mobility and broadband service revenue was $22.9 billion, up 1.6% (including the 80bp outage drag). Postpaid phone net adds were 55,000 — the first positive Q1 figure in 13 years, up 344,000 year over year. Consumer postpaid phone churn was 0.90% in the quarter, improving to below 0.85% by March. Prepaid net adds were 115,000 (Visible and Total Wireless brands). Broadband net adds were 341,000 (214,000 fixed wireless access, 127,000 fiber), bringing total broadband subscribers to 16.8 million. Cost of acquisition and retention fell about 35% in March versus end of Q4. Adjusted EPS was $1.28, up 7.6% year over year — the highest adjusted EPS growth rate in over four years. Consolidated adjusted EBITDA was $13.4 billion, up 6.7%, with margin expanding 140 basis points to 38.9% (a record). Free cash flow was $3.8 billion, up 4%; full-year guidance of approximately 7%+ growth and $21.5 billion or more was reaffirmed. Capex was $4.2 billion in the quarter; full-year guidance $16-16.5 billion, targeting over 32 million fiber passings by year-end. Verizon raised full-year adjusted EPS growth guidance to 5%-6% (from 4%-5%) and now expects postpaid phone net adds in the upper half of the 750,000-1 million range. The company completed $2.5 billion in share buybacks in the quarter and raised its annualized dividend by $0.07/share (+2.5%), the 20th consecutive year of increases. Frontier integration remains on track for over $1 billion in run-rate operating cost synergies by 2028.

--- CEO PREPARED REMARKS ---
CEO Daniel Schulman framed the quarter as evidence Verizon's turnaround "is not only progressing, it is gaining momentum," citing the first positive Q1 postpaid phone net adds in 13 years and the East unions' new four-year contract ratification. He said the company is "purposely shifting our mix towards durable recurring service revenues and away from low-margin, highly promotional activity" and "prioritizing customer lifetime value over short-term revenue maximization." He detailed a 10-work-stream company-wide transformation program covering AI-first operations, customer friction reduction, and micro-segmentation, and said Verizon is "in the final stages of extensive market research that will inform a new generation of offers built around the principles of transparency, simplicity and genuine value delivery." Schulman raised full-year adjusted EPS growth guidance to 5%-6% and said postpaid phone net adds should land in the upper half of the 750,000-1 million guided range, while reaffirming 2%-3% mobility/broadband revenue growth and ~7%+ free cash flow growth.

--- CFO PREPARED REMARKS ---
CFO Anthony Skiadas said Q1 marked "the first time since 2013" Verizon generated positive total postpaid phone net additions, with consumer postpaid phone net losses of 35,000 representing a 321,000 improvement year over year. Total postpaid phone churn fell 5 basis points sequentially to 0.97%, with consumer churn at 0.90%. He confirmed wireless service revenue of $20.6 billion (down 1% YoY) with the outage-related 80bp drag, and reiterated confidence in full-year revenue guidance. Adjusted EBITDA hit $13.4 billion (record, +6.7%), and adjusted EPS reached $1.28 (+7.6%), "even as we incurred the incremental depreciation and interest expense associated with the Frontier acquisition." Operating cash flow was $8 billion despite absorbing the $1.1 billion severance charge. He confirmed the company has repaid "about half of the Frontier debt since the acquisition closed" and expects to repay substantially all of it by year-end, targeting the 2.0-2.25x leverage range during 2027.

--- ANALYST Q&A EXCERPT (risk / demand relevant) ---
Michael Rollins (Citi) asked about account/ARPA trends and how the promotional environment is influencing pricing strategy. Schulman said the "majority of the ARPA decline in the first quarter came from our decision to do the right thing for customers on the network outage and to immediately give them credits," but called this "a onetime event" that "doesn't come and follow us through the rest of the year," expecting account net adds and ARPA to continue improving through 2026 and 2027.

Michael Ng (Goldman Sachs) asked about upgrade rates and changing device-subsidy strategy. Schulman said cost-of-acquisition and cost-of-retention improvements "are structural changes... not onetime or seasonal," driven by micro-segmentation rather than automatically offering free handsets for retention — giving an example where a customer with a home coverage complaint previously received "a free handset" that cost roughly $1,000 without solving the underlying service issue, illustrating the shift toward more disciplined, needs-based retention spending.
