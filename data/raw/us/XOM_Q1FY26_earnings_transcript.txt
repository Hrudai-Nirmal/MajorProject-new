--- RISKS (flagged by Motley Fool editorial) ---
CEO Darren Woods said repair time for the two LNG trains in Qatar damaged during the Middle East conflict "will be anywhere between three and five years," representing about 3% of the company's global production. Woods noted the market "has not seen the full impact" of the regional disruption yet, and that as strategic and commercial inventories are drawn down further, this "will put upward pressure on prices." External shocks — the Middle East conflict, drone attacks in Kazakhstan, and a January Permian winter storm — all hit first-quarter Upstream production. On insurance exposure to the Qatar damage, Woods said he does not "see any material impacts with respect to Cutter and the insurance portfolio," while acknowledging the company still relies heavily on self-insurance. CFO Kathy Mikells flagged "temporary, transient impacts" in the quarter's financial results tied to identified/timing items.

--- TAKEAWAYS ---
Energy Products segment earnings were $2.8 billion for the quarter, up $2 billion year over year and up a few hundred million dollars sequentially versus Q4 2025. Excluding external disruptions (Middle East, Kazakhstan, Permian winter storm), Upstream production rose 8% year over year, driven by growth in the Permian and Guyana. Gulf Coast refineries ran at record utilization; refinery throughput rose ~200,000 barrels per day from February to March as deferred maintenance was expedited back online. Exxon is on track for 1.8 million oil-equivalent barrels/day in Permian output for full-year 2026. Golden Pass LNG achieved first LNG in March; Train 1 lifts US LNG exports ~5% versus 2025, rising to roughly 15% once all three trains are online (Train 2 mechanically complete by year-end 2026, Train 3 by Q2 2027). The Beaumont refinery expansion (completed 2023) has fully paid back its investment ahead of schedule. Excluding identified items and timing effects, Q1 EPS was up versus Q1 2025. Guyana hit record production with Oahu, Whiptail and Hammerhead projects under construction (Oahu first oil expected late 2026). Mozambique and Papua New Guinea LNG projects are expected to reach final investment decisions later in 2026.

--- CEO PREPARED REMARKS ---
Darren Woods opened by acknowledging the human toll of the Middle East conflict on colleagues and regional partners, saying "the financial impact in the region is real, [but] what is even more real is the daily threat our colleagues and partners have been living under." He argued Exxon's "scale, integration, and execution excellence" allowed it to respond to supply disruptions — rerouting Gulf Coast-to-Asia cargoes, maximizing refinery throughput, and growing Permian and Guyana output. He highlighted Golden Pass LNG's first cargo in March, continued progress toward FID on Mozambique and Papua New Guinea LNG, record Guyana production with a $100 million, ten-year STEM education commitment there, and confirmation that Permian growth remains "grounded in value, not volume," targeting 1.8 million oil-equivalent barrels/day for 2026. He also cited the Beaumont refinery expansion fully recovering its investment, a Low Carbon Solutions CO2 transport-and-storage startup (its second in under a year), first deepwater fully autonomous well section technology in Guyana, and the launch of a major enterprise workforce system across 50+ countries "with no business disruption."

--- CFO PREPARED REMARKS ---
Kathy Mikells reinforced that excluding external disruptions (Middle East, Kazakhstan drone attacks, Permian winter storm), year-over-year Upstream production was up 8%, driven by "advantaged assets" in the Permian and Guyana. She said this "highlights the benefit and value of having a global, diverse portfolio." She quantified Energy Products segment earnings at $2.8 billion for the quarter, "up $2 billion compared to last year, and a few hundred million dollars compared to the fourth quarter," crediting newly onlined world-class assets and expanded trading capability. She also noted the company's North American-weighted refining and chemicals footprint carries a structural feedstock/energy cost advantage as oil prices rise.

--- ANALYST Q&A EXCERPT (risk / demand relevant) ---
Devin McDermott (Morgan Stanley) pressed on the near- and long-term market impact of the Middle East disruption. Woods responded that once the Strait reopens, "it will take some time to get back to a stable flow rate," estimating "a one- to two-month time lag between the Strait opening up and the market seeing normal flow," with replenishment of drawn-down strategic and commercial inventories likely adding "upward pressure on prices." On the Qatar LNG trains specifically, Woods said, "Cutter Energy came out very early on and said the repair time will be anywhere between three and five years... we are working to be on the low end of that range, but we have more work to do to fully assess the damage."

Jean Ann Salisbury (Bank of America) asked what drives the three- to five-year repair range and whether cold-box lead times (reportedly two to three years) are the bottleneck. Woods confirmed "thinking of it in those terms is accurate" but said he has "not accepted any specific schedule because we have not been able to do all the work we need to do." He added confidence that "whatever timeline we set, I do not think there would be anybody else who could beat it."
