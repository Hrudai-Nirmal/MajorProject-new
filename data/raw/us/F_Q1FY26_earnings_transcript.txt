--- RISKS (flagged by Motley Fool editorial) ---
Management stated explicitly: "Our guidance does not include the potential impacts of a sustained conflict in the Middle East or a significant downturn in the U.S. economy, which could have a material impact on industry demand." CFO Sherry House flagged "commodity headwinds of just above $2 billion, about $1 billion higher than our previous estimate, largely due to higher aluminum pricing driven by global supply constraints" (excluding Novelis-related costs). Ford also faces $1.5-2 billion in one-time incremental costs for alternative aluminum sourcing until its Novelis supplier is fully restarted. Adjusted free cash flow was a use of $1.9 billion in the quarter, "more than explained by unfavorable timing differences, higher net spending, and changes in working capital."

--- TAKEAWAYS ---
Revenue was $43.3 billion, up over 6% year over year despite a nearly 4% volume decline from exiting low-margin nameplates (Escape in North America, Focus in Europe). Adjusted EBIT was $3.5 billion, or $2.2 billion excluding a one-time $1.3 billion IEPA tariff benefit ($700 million to Ford Blue, $500 million to Ford Pro). Full-year adjusted EBIT guidance was raised to $8.5-10.5 billion. Segment results: Ford Pro EBIT $1.7 billion with paid software subscriptions at 879,000 (+30% YoY); Ford Blue EBIT $1.9 billion, full-year guidance raised $500 million to $4.5-5 billion; Ford Model e loss of $777 million, a nearly 35% improvement in Gen 1 losses; Ford Credit EBT $783 million, up $200 million. Company is targeting another $1 billion in material/warranty cost reductions in 2026 (on top of $1.5 billion delivered in 2025). Balance sheet: $22 billion cash, over $43 billion total liquidity. Q2 dividend of $0.15/share declared, payable June 1 to holders of record May 12. Full-year FCF guidance held at $5-6 billion; capex guided to $9.5-10.5 billion, including $1.5 billion for Ford Energy.

--- CEO PREPARED REMARKS ---
James Farley opened by crediting "a strong start to this year," citing $43.3 billion in revenue and $3.5 billion in adjusted EBIT, and announced the raised full-year guidance of $8.5-10.5 billion. He described a newly unified organization merging advanced technology, digital, design and industrial teams, saying "by 2030, almost all of our global volume will feature next-generation electric architectures and in-house software." He highlighted the Skunk Works-developed universal EV (UEV) platform launching from Louisville Assembly Plant in 2027, an 80% refresh of the North America portfolio and 70% of global volume by 2029, and software/physical-services revenue (over $15 billion last year) targeted to grow "nearly 8% annually through the end of the decade." On geopolitics, Farley said Ford is "totally dedicated to a thriving U.S. auto industry and safeguarding our country's industrial base," while remaining open to selective global partnerships, including IP sharing with Chinese OEMs outside the U.S. He also cited Ford's best J.D. Power Customer Service Index ranking (No. 4) in 30 years.

--- CFO PREPARED REMARKS ---
Sherry House detailed the $1.3 billion IEPA tariff benefit and confirmed the Novelis aluminum supplier recovery remains on track for "a $1 billion improvement in EBIT year-over-year, weighted towards the second half," net of $1.5-2 billion in temporary alternative-sourcing costs. She noted U.S. retail inventory should stay within the 55-65 day target range, and that F-150 had "the highest retail share, the highest average transaction price, and the lowest incentive spend per unit" versus key competitors. She confirmed the $1.9 billion free cash flow use in the quarter but said full-year timing and working capital effects should turn favorable, keeping FCF guidance at $5-6 billion. She reiterated commodity headwinds of "just above $2 billion, about $1 billion higher than our previous estimate," and that tariff impact excluding IEPA and Novelis remains about $1 billion, now treated as a run-rate cost.

--- ANALYST Q&A EXCERPT (risk / demand relevant) ---
Joseph Spak (UBS) pressed on the size of the commodity cost increase and Novelis restart timing. COO Kumar Galhotra confirmed: "We are still expecting the hot mill to restart in May... In the event the relaunch does not go according to plan, we have contingency plans in place... additional aluminum supply to ensure our plant production schedules are not interrupted." Farley added Ford tracks aluminum "by grade" daily and has built backup supply in case the mill ramps slower than planned.

Emmanuel Rosner (Wolfe Research) questioned the sharply lower implied earnings cadence for the rest of the year (roughly $6 billion combined over Q2-Q4 versus $3.5 billion in Q1) and the $1.9 billion free cash flow burn. House explained the bridge: no repeat of the $1.3 billion IEPA benefit, Novelis-related sourcing costs tapering only in the back half, greater commodity headwinds later in the year, and rising launch investment in UEV and Ford Energy — partially offset by Novelis volume recovery. She said full-year timing and working-capital effects are still expected to turn favorable, supporting the maintained $5-6 billion FCF guide.
