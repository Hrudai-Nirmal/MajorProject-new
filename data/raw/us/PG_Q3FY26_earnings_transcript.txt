--- RISKS (flagged by Motley Fool editorial) ---
CFO Andre Schulten flagged a "$1 billion after tax" (approximately $1.3 billion pre-tax) cost headwind from commodity inflation, feedstock exposure, and Middle East-related logistics disruption, saying "almost all of these increased costs will be in the fiscal fourth quarter." Management now expects "full-year EPS results to be toward the lower end of the guidance range." Guidance also includes a roughly $500 million pre-tax tariff headwind for the year, with no assumption of material recovery. Schulten noted the annual cost impact of Brent crude near $100/barrel is roughly "$1.3 billion before tax, or $1 billion after tax," versus a pre-conflict price in the mid-sixties, and disclosed "some force majeure declarations by our direct suppliers or by their upstream suppliers." Core operating margin fell 80 basis points and gross margin fell 100 basis points year over year, driven by reinvestment and the Middle East-related energy/cost impacts.

--- TAKEAWAYS ---
Organic sales grew more than 3% (volume +2 points, pricing +1 point, mix flat), with growth across all 10 product categories and all seven regions. North America organic sales rose 4% (volume +3 points), helped by a trade-inventory bump tied to Easter timing. Europe was up 2% (enterprise markets +6%); Greater China grew 3%, led by Pampers and SK-II each up double digits. Latin America was up 5% (Mexico, Brazil each high single digits). Skin and Personal Care grew high single digits; Hair Care, Family Care, and Home Care grew mid-single digits. Core EPS was $1.59, up 3% (flat on a currency-neutral basis). Global exit market share was in line with prior year, with 26 of the top 50 category-country pairs holding or gaining share. Adjusted free cash flow productivity was 82% for the quarter (full-year guide 85%-90%). The company returned $3.2 billion to shareholders in the quarter ($2.5 billion dividends, $600+ million buybacks) and raised its dividend 3% — the 70th consecutive annual increase and 136th consecutive year of dividend payments. Full-year FY2026 guidance was maintained: organic sales growth "in line to 4%," core EPS growth "in line to 4%" (a range of $6.83-$7.09/share, expected at the lower end), and plans to return roughly $15 billion in cash to shareholders ($10 billion dividends, $5 billion buybacks). Restructuring remains on track for a 15% non-manufacturing headcount reduction over two years.

--- CEO PREPARED REMARKS ---
No CEO participated on this call; CFO Andre Schulten led the entire presentation and Q&A alongside Investor Relations SVPs John Chevalier and Kerry Cohen.

--- CFO PREPARED REMARKS ---
Andre Schulten said the quarter showed "solid acceleration in top-line results," with broad-based growth across all categories and regions, though bottom-line results reflected "partial offsets from incremental investments in the business and energy cost impacts from the conflict in the Middle East." He cited innovation wins including Fairy Skip the Soak in the U.K. (household penetration up five points to 61%) and Mr. Clean's new Magic Eraser and Shower/Tub Scrubber lines, plus continued Supply Chain 3.0 automation rollout. On guidance, he reiterated the $150 million after-tax Middle East-related cost headwind concentrated in fiscal Q4, the $500 million pre-tax tariff headwind, and said the company would "protect our demand creation investments" even at the cost of near-term margin, adding "we remain willing to manage some short-term pressure on the bottom line to come out of this period with stronger brands and business momentum on the other side."

--- ANALYST Q&A EXCERPT (risk / demand relevant) ---
Steve Powers (Deutsche Bank) asked whether productivity alone could offset the ~$1 billion after-tax headwind or whether pricing would also be needed heading into fiscal 2027. Schulten replied: "there is a lot of room in our P&L to drive short-term productivity, and that would be the first place to go. Will it be sufficient to offset the full $1 billion after tax? Likely not... selective pricing with innovation... will be the other part of the offset."

Filippo Falorni (Citi) asked about Middle East conflict demand impact in Asia/Middle East/Africa enterprise markets. Schulten said the Middle East itself is "about 2%" of global sales and reported no direct demand hit: "The direct impact on sales—no; the business is still doing well," while noting upstream supply chain exposure is heavier in Southeast Asia, where the team is working to secure feedstock and materials.
