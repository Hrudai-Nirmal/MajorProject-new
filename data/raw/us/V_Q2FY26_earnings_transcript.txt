--- RISKS (flagged by Motley Fool editorial) ---
CEMEA (Central Europe, Middle East, Africa) payments volume growth stepped down about 2.5 percentage points versus Q1, "primarily due to the conflict in the Middle East," per CFO Christopher Suh, though he noted CEMEA is only about 6% of total payments volume. Guidance now reflects "some near-term uncertainty, in particular to cross-border travel spend in the CEMEA region." Cross-border travel volume growth in April was affected by both the Middle East conflict and Ramadan timing, though normalizing for Ramadan, April growth was in line with February levels. Non-operating expense rose to $45 million, above expectations, "primarily due to lower cash balances and higher debt levels and interest rates than forecasted," and full-year non-operating expense guidance was raised to approximately $150 million.

--- TAKEAWAYS ---
Net revenue was $11.2 billion, up 17% year over year — the fastest growth since 2022, and (excluding post-pandemic recovery and the Visa Europe acquisition) the fastest since 2013. EPS was $3.31, up 20% year over year in both nominal and constant dollars, including about a 0.5-point currency benefit. Payments volume reached $3.7 trillion, up 9% year over year in constant dollars; processed transactions were 66 billion, up 9% (about 900 million transactions/day). U.S. payments volume grew 8% (credit +10%, debit +7%); international payments volume grew 10% in constant dollars. Cross-border volume (ex. intra-Europe) rose 11%, with e-commerce up 13% and travel up 10%. Client incentives grew 14% (below expectations on deal timing). Value-Added Services (VAS) revenue was $3.3 billion, 30% of net revenue, up 27% in constant dollars. Commercial and Money Movement Solutions (CMS) revenue grew 24% in constant dollars; commercial payments volume rose 11%. Visa Direct processed 3.7 billion transactions, up 23%. Stablecoin-linked card programs exceeded 160, with volume up nearly 200% year over year; stablecoin settlement reached a $7 billion annual run rate, up more than 50% sequentially, across 9 blockchains. Operating expenses grew 17%. The company repurchased $7.9 billion of stock in the quarter — the largest buyback in company history — and the board authorized a new $20 billion program, bringing total authorization to roughly $33 billion; $1.3 billion in dividends was also paid. Full-year FY2026 guidance was raised: net revenue and EPS growth now expected in the low double digits to low teens, with adjusted EPS growth revised up to the low teens.

--- CEO PREPARED REMARKS ---
CEO Ryan McInerney called Visa "the leading hyperscaler of payments globally," framing growth around four drivers: winning in consumer, commercial, and money-movement payments; AI/agentic commerce expanding the addressable market (citing a third-party estimate of 80-150 basis points of incremental global GDP growth from AI); stablecoins/blockchain, where Visa has become "a key interoperability layer," now settling on 9 blockchains with a $7 billion annualized stablecoin settlement run rate; and value-added services, "an even bigger opportunity," now 30% of net revenue and growing over 25% in constant dollars. He cited new partnerships (TikTok Creator Card in the U.K., PayPay in Japan, X Money in the U.S., UnionPay International in China), the Wells Fargo agreement to migrate to Pismo's core account ledger, and said the company is "watching the impacts from the conflict in the Middle East closely," with the safety of Visa's team and clients as the primary concern.

--- CFO PREPARED REMARKS ---
CFO Christopher Suh confirmed net revenue up 17% (16% in constant dollars) and EPS up 20%, driven by "higher-than-expected volatility, stronger-than-expected value-added services revenue and lower-than-expected incentives." He detailed the CEMEA step-down "primarily due to the conflict in the Middle East" and noted travel-related cross-border volume growth of 10% was helped by commercial strength and improved U.S. inbound volume that "generally offset the impact in the Middle East that was most pronounced in March." He confirmed the record $7.9 billion buyback and the new $20 billion authorization. On guidance, he said the Middle East conflict "has introduced some near-term uncertainty, in particular to cross-border travel spend in the CEMEA region," while expecting offsetting strength from FIFA World Cup-driven U.S. and Latin America inbound travel later in the year.

--- ANALYST Q&A EXCERPT (risk / demand relevant) ---
Bryan Keane (Citi) asked about the Middle East conflict's lingering impact on cross-border growth heading into Q3, referencing an April deceleration. Suh replied: "while there is some impact in the Middle East as we saw in Q2, and we do expect to see in Q3, we've seen that there's offsetting factors, strength in other regions, other parts of the business," pointing to expected U.S. and Latin America inbound travel gains tied to the upcoming FIFA World Cup and continued cross-border e-commerce strength.

Craig Maurer (FT Partners) asked how Visa would manage agentic-commerce fraud risk across its four-party network model. McInerney said, "If a Visa cardholder experiences fraud, they're going to be protected. That's been part of the promise for Visa cardholders for a very, very long time," and noted authenticated-token transactions in agentic commerce should generate more data to strengthen dispute and risk processes as the ecosystem matures.
