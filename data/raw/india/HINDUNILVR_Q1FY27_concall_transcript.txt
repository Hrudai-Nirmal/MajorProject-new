--- FINANCIAL RESULTS ---
For the quarter ended 30 June 2026, HUL's consolidated turnover rose 10.8% YoY to approximately Rs 17,149-17,184 crore (figures vary slightly by source), its highest quarterly growth in 13 quarters, driven by underlying sales growth (USG) of 10% split evenly with underlying volume growth (UVG) of 5%. Consolidated net profit attributable to owners declined 3-4% YoY to roughly Rs 2,631-2,680 crore, a decline management attributed to a one-off tax credit in the base (year-ago) quarter rather than to weaker operating performance. EBITDA grew 8% YoY to Rs 3,947 crore, with operating margin holding at 23.0%, within management's target band. Shares fell over 3% following the results.

--- GUIDANCE / RISK SIGNAL ---
Palm oil and packaging input costs squeezed margins during the quarter. CEO Rohit Jawa told analysts commodity pressures are expected to ease in the second half of FY27 as crude oil prices moderate, and management expressed confidence that FY27 overall will be better than FY26. However, management also acknowledged that volume growth, while real, came in below internal targets, and that the company had taken price increases in select categories but was constrained in pushing further without risking demand destruction — an explicit demand-elasticity risk signal.

--- MANAGEMENT COMMENTARY ---
CEO Rohit Jawa characterized the quarter as broad-based volume-led growth across the portfolio and expressed confidence in navigating continued market/input-cost volatility, with an expectation that FY27 will outperform FY26. Management's commentary on pricing constraints ("volume growth had... fallen below internal targets... constrained in how far it could push further without risking demand destruction") is a notable candid risk disclosure rather than pure promotional framing.
